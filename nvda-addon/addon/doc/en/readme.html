Titan enhancements

Makes NVDA a first-class citizen of the Titan desktop. Titan does not have to be running: with no Titan this add-on does nothing at all and costs nothing.

What it does

Titan's announcements arrive with their structure intact

Titan's interface is built to be heard. A control on the left of the shell's taskbar sounds on the left; a row of a Cling board is lower and quieter the further away it is; an announcement carries a role, a state and a place in a list. Titan's own reader gets all of that, and NVDA used to get a flat string through the NVDA controller - or, worse, nothing at all, because Titan deliberately stayed silent rather than say a second, worse copy of what NVDA was about to read anyway.

With this add-on the position, the pitch, the rate and the volume are applied to whichever synthesizer you are using (by setting the per-channel volume of the stream underneath it), and an announcement that Titan marks as replacing a control's report suppresses NVDA's own report once instead of racing it. A Titan voice is offered as one more synthesizer, but is never required.

Titan's subsystems know what you are looking at

NVDA knows which window it is reading, which control has the focus, what its role and state are, what is selected and where the review cursor is. Titan cannot work any of that out, because Titan is not the reader. So the channel runs both ways: AI OCR reads the window NVDA is in rather than whatever Windows calls the foreground, and a question put to Titan's assistant carries what you are actually on.

Titan's subsystems become NVDA commands

Everything Titan can do - its AI OCR, its assistant, its macros, and every action of every add-on installed in it - is offered to NVDA as a script of its own. They are in NVDA's own Input Gestures dialog, grouped per Titan add-on, so you bind whichever you use to whatever you like: a key, a touch gesture, a braille display button. NVDA remembers the binding in your own profile, and it survives Titan not running - a key pressed then says so.

Titan's AI can reach NVDA

Behind switches of your own, Titan's assistant, agent, macros and Titan Scripts can speak in NVDA's voice, stop it, move its review cursor, change its rate or its synthesizer, and put it into browse or focus mode. Reading NVDA is always served; changing it is one switch; pressing NVDA's own commands is a second switch, off by default - a gesture is whatever you bound it to, and in a document a bound key can delete something.

Commands

GestureWhat it does
NVDA+shift+tThe Titan menu: everything Titan can do
NVDA+alt+tWhether Titan is connected and what it can send
NVDA+alt+iAsk Titan's AI assistant a question
NVDA+alt+shift+iTell Titan's AI to do something, and let it act
NVDA+alt+oRead the current window with Titan's AI OCR
NVDA+alt+qAsk Titan's AI a question about the current window
NVDA+alt+shift+oPut Titan's AI OCR overlay over the current window
NVDA+alt+mRun one of Titan's macros
NVDA+alt+aRun anything Titan or one of its add-ons can do
NVDA+alt+pPositioned speech for Titan on and off
NVDA+alt+nTitan's own announcements on and off

Everything else - and every Titan action - is unbound and waiting for you in Input Gestures, under the Titan categories.

Settings

NVDA Settings, Titan. Kept in NVDA rather than in Titan on purpose: they are answers about how NVDA should behave, they must be readable when Titan is not running, and uninstalling Titan should not leave your reader configured by a program that is gone.

Requirements

Windows, NVDA 2023.3 or newer, and Titan. Positioned speech needs an NVDA whose audio layer can set the volume of one channel of a stream; where it cannot, the add-on says so and marks the position with a tone instead.
